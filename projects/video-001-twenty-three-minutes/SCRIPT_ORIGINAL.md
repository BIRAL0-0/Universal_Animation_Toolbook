## S01 — THE COUNTER
*Bedroom, early morning. KAI wakes. A number appears in the air beside him: 240.*

**NARRATOR:** Kai wakes up on a Tuesday morning, and there is a number floating above his head.
**NARRATOR:** 240. That's his focus. That's every minute of attention he gets today.
**KAI:** What is that?
**NARRATOR:** He can't see it. But it's already counting down.

## S02 — THE RULE
*Neutral mind-space. KAI paces. Two stat cards: 2 SECONDS / 23 MINUTES.*

**NARRATOR:** Here's the rule nobody tells you.
**NARRATOR:** A notification takes about 2 seconds to read.
**NARRATOR:** It takes 23 minutes to come back from.
**NARRATOR:** That's not a metaphor. Researchers at UC Irvine measured it, in real offices, and the average was 23 minutes and 15 seconds.

## S03 — FIRST DRAIN
*Office, 9:00 AM. KAI at his desk. The phone lights up. Counter: 240 → 217.*

**NARRATOR:** At 9:00 AM, his phone lights up on the desk.
**NARRATOR:** He reads it, smiles, and goes back to work. Or he thinks he goes back to work.
**KAI:** Two seconds. That's nothing.
**NARRATOR:** 240 becomes 217. He doesn't feel a thing, and that's exactly the problem.

## S04 — ESCALATION
*Same office, later. Three more buzzes. Counter: 217 → 194 → 171.*

**NARRATOR:** Then another. And another. And another.
**NARRATOR:** 217. 194. 171.
**NARRATOR:** Each one felt free. None of them were.
**NARRATOR:** He isn't losing seconds. He's losing the return trip, every single time.

## S05 — THE ARITHMETIC
*Tech-lab white space. A bar chart builds: 11 × 23 MIN.*

**NARRATOR:** By lunch, Kai has answered 11 notifications.
**NARRATOR:** 11 times 23 minutes is more than 4 hours.
**NARRATOR:** He started with 8.
**NARRATOR:** The work on his desk hasn't moved an inch.

## S06 — EMPTY AFTERNOON
*Dusk at the desk. KAI slumped, head in hands. Counter: 009.*

**NARRATOR:** At 3:00 PM, the counter reads 9.
**NARRATOR:** Nothing dramatic happened. No crash. No disaster.
**KAI:** Where did the day go?
**NARRATOR:** It went into the gaps between things.

## S07 — THE REAL THIEF
*Mind-space. A flow chain builds: TASK → BUZZ → TASK → TASK → BACK.*

**NARRATOR:** Here's the part that surprised the researchers.
**NARRATOR:** Nobody goes straight back to what they were doing.
**NARRATOR:** Two other tasks slip in first. Every single time.
**NARRATOR:** So the phone didn't steal his time. The switching did.

## S08 — ONE SMALL THING
*Office. KAI turns the phone face down and puts it in the drawer. A declined-call card.*

**NARRATOR:** So Kai does one small thing.
**NARRATOR:** He turns the phone face down, and puts it in the drawer.
**NARRATOR:** Nothing dramatic. Nothing heroic. Just out of sight.

## S09 — ONE HOUR LATER
*Office, warm light. KAI deep at work. SAM passes. Counter: 217 — unchanged.*

**NARRATOR:** One hour later, the counter hasn't moved.
**NARRATOR:** 217 minutes, still sitting there.
**SAM:** You finished it?
**KAI:** I started it. And I stayed.

## S10 — THE PRICE
*Sunrise summit. KAI looks out. A single checklist card.*

**NARRATOR:** Your attention was never free.
**NARRATOR:** Every buzz has a price, and you pay it in minutes you don't get back.
**NARRATOR:** Scientists watched our focus on a single screen fall from 2.5 minutes to 47 seconds.
**NARRATOR:** So before you unlock your phone, ask one question.
**NARRATOR:** What is this going to cost me?

## Source note (for the end card)

Focus-recovery figure: Mark, G., Gudith, D., & Klocke, U. (2008), *The Cost of Interrupted Work:
More Speed and Stress*, UC Irvine — 23 min 15 s average. Screen-attention figure: Gloria Mark,
*Attention Span* (2023) — 2.5 min (2004) → 47 s (2023). Full evidence table and confidence
grading: [`RESEARCH.md`](RESEARCH.md) §5.

The "240 minutes of focus in a day" budget is an **explicit hypothetical** used to make the
mechanism visible. It is not a measured quantity and the narration does not present it as one.
