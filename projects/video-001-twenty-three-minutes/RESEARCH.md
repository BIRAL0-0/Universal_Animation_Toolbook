> **Caveat, stated plainly:** Shorts and long-form videos draw from different recommendation

| three further Shorts with near-identical titles | short | 1.3K / 1.9K / 2.6K | clustered low performers |

**[O] This is a saturated title pattern.** "What if you quit your phone for N days" has a 1.7M
live-action incumbent, a 44K Short, and a wall of 1–3K Shorts. The *calendar countdown* structure
specifically is well-mined: at least two competitors already use Day 1 / Day 7 / Day 21 / Day 30
chapter beats.

**Gap identified [P]:** the crowded videos all measure the phone in **days of abstinence**.
Almost none of them name the *mechanism* — what a single interruption actually costs, in minutes,
at the moment it happens. That mechanism is both more surprising and more actionable than "quit
for 30 days", and it has not been claimed by a breakout animated short.

## 5. Fact base for the selected concept

All verified on 2026-11-09 from multiple independent secondary sources reporting the primary
literature. Primary source: **Mark, G., Gudith, D., & Klocke, U. (2008), "The Cost of Interrupted
Work: More Speed and Stress"**, UC Irvine.

| Claim | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Time to fully return to a task after an interruption | **23 min 15 s** average | High — most-cited figure in the field, consistently attributed to Mark et al. 2008 |
| Sustained attention on a single screen | **2.5 min (2004) → 47 s (2023)** | High — Mark, *Attention Span* (2023) |
| Intervening tasks before returning to the original work | **~2 tasks** on average | Medium-High — reported consistently across summaries of Mark's observational data |
| Interruptions per knowledge worker per day | **~275** (one every ~2 min in core hours) | Medium — Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025, as reported by secondary sources |
| Share of interruptions that are self-initiated | **~44%** | Medium — reported as Mark's finding |
| Daily time lost to distraction | **~1 h 18 min/day (~340 h/yr)** | Medium — secondary aggregation, not used in the script |

**Script policy adopted from this table:** only the **High** and **Medium-High** rows appear as
assertions in the narration. The Medium rows are left out rather than risk overstating them. The
"240 minutes of focus a day" figure is **not** presented as a measured fact — the script frames
the whole premise as an explicit thought experiment ("let's say"), because no credible source
gives a universal daily focus budget.

## 6. Audience and discovery

**Audience [P]:** 16–34, mobile-first, self-improvement/productivity adjacent. The same audience
that made *DOPAMINE* break out. They arrive from the Shorts shelf and from search on
"dopamine detox", "can't focus", "phone addiction".

**Search/discovery interest [O]:** queries in this cluster return both Shorts and 2–4 minute
"animated short film" results, and the long-form results are the ones carrying six-figure view
counts. Both surfaces reward the same title grammar: *What If / What Happens* + a concrete
surprising quantity.

**Fit for stickman storytelling [P]:** very high. The concept is a *visible counter* — a number
that drains on screen. That is exactly what a minimal vector character with an LED-clock prop and
stat-card boards can communicate better than live action could, because the abstraction is the
point.
